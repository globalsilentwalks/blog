Paris Travel Guide 2026 — The City of Light

Paris, the City of Light, is one of the most iconic destinations on the planet. Whether you are visiting for the first time, returning for the hundredth, or moving here as a student, the French capital offers an unmatched mix of history, art, gastronomy and modern lifestyle. From the Eiffel Tower glittering at dusk to the cobblestone streets of Montmartre, Paris feels like a living museum where every neighborhood tells its own story.

Best places to visit in Paris

  • Eiffel Tower & Champ de Mars — Climb to the summit at sunset for the best panorama of the city, then picnic on the lawn below.
  • Louvre Museum — Home to the Mona Lisa and 35,000+ works. Book online and arrive at 9 a.m. to skip the worst lines.
  • Notre-Dame & Île de la Cité — Reopened after restoration, the cathedral and its surrounding island are unmissable.
  • Montmartre & Sacré-Cœur — Wander up the hill, watch artists at work on Place du Tertre and end with the city's best free view.
  • Le Marais — The trendiest district for boutiques, falafel, vintage shops and LGBTQ+ nightlife.
  • Latin Quarter & Sorbonne — The historic student heart of Paris — bookshops, cheap eats and lively cafés.

Top tourist attractions

  • Musée d'Orsay (Impressionist masterpieces in a former train station)
  • Sainte-Chapelle (the most stunning stained glass in Europe)
  • Palais Garnier opera house
  • Centre Pompidou (modern art + skyline view)
  • Père-Lachaise Cemetery (Jim Morrison, Édith Piaf, Oscar Wilde)
  • Versailles day trip (RER C — 45 min from central Paris)

Student life in Paris

Paris is a global student capital with over 600,000 students enrolled in institutions like Sorbonne, Sciences Po, HEC and PSL. International students benefit from CAF housing aid (up to €200/month), the Pass Navigo Imagine R card (€1 a day for unlimited transport under 26), university restaurants (CROUS) serving full meals for €3.30, and free entry to most national museums under 26 from the EU. The CAF aid alone can cover a third of your rent — apply within your first month of arrival.

Transportation tips

The Paris Métro (14 lines) is the fastest way to move around — buy a Navigo Easy card and load weekly passes (€30.75) or single tickets (€2.15). RER trains link the airports (CDG and Orly) to the center in 35–50 min. Vélib' bikes (€3/day pass) and Lime e-scooters are great for shorter trips. Avoid driving — parking is brutal and many central streets are now pedestrian-only.

Budget tips

Budget travelers can survive on €60–80 per day (hostel + Métro + bakery lunches + 1 sit-down dinner). Mid-range comfort sits around €130–180. Tap water is excellent and free at any restaurant — always ask for 'une carafe d'eau'. The Paris Museum Pass (€70 for 4 days) pays for itself in 3 visits.

Food & restaurant recommendations

  • Boulangeries: a fresh croissant should cost €1.20–€1.80, not €4.
  • Bouillon Pigalle / Bouillon Chartier: traditional French menus around €15.
  • L'As du Fallafel in Le Marais — €10 falafel, worth the queue.
  • Marché des Enfants Rouges (oldest covered market in Paris).
  • A perfect weekend ritual: wine + cheese + baguette on the Canal Saint-Martin.

Hidden gems most travelers miss

  • The Promenade Plantée — an elevated park built on an old railway, the original of New York's High Line.
  • Rue Crémieux — a tiny pastel-colored street most tourists never find.
  • Square du Vert-Galant at the western tip of Île de la Cité — sunsets over the Seine.
  • Musée de la Vie Romantique — free, with a hidden garden tea room.
  • The Covered Passages (Galerie Vivienne, Passage des Panoramas) — 19th-century shopping arcades.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Paris?

4 to 5 days is the sweet spot for first-timers — enough to cover the major monuments, two museums, a Versailles day trip and still have time to wander the neighborhoods at human pace.

Is Paris safe for solo travelers and students?

Yes. The center is generally very safe, even at night. Stay alert for pickpockets in the Métro, around the Eiffel Tower and Sacré-Cœur. Avoid empty Métro cars after midnight.

What's the best time of year to visit Paris?

Late April to June, and September to mid-October. Mild weather, fewer tourists than July–August, and the gardens are at their best.

Do I need to speak French?

You can get by with English in central Paris, but learning a few phrases (bonjour, merci, s'il vous plaît) instantly transforms how locals treat you.

How much does it cost to study in Paris as an international student?

Public universities charge around €2,800–3,800 per year for non-EU students; private schools much more. Budget €1,100–1,500/month for living costs (after CAF housing aid).

Conclusion

Paris rewards slow travel. Skip the urge to tick off every monument and instead let yourself get lost in a neighborhood, sit at a café, and watch the city breathe. Whether you stay for a long weekend or for an entire degree, Paris will leave its imprint on you forever.
